## 1. Circuit Objective

The objective of **Circuit B** is to implement and validate the second gain stage of a two-stage CMOS operational amplifier architecture: a **Common-Source NMOS Amplifier with an Active PMOS Current-Source Load and Miller Frequency Compensation ($C_C$)**.

Circuit B is designed to operate as a completely autonomous, independently simulatable subsystem. It interfaces small-signal voltages from a high-impedance driver (such as Circuit A, the CMOS Differential Input Stage) and provides:
1. High open-circuit voltage gain ($A_v > 45\text{ dB}$, $\sim 250\text{ V/V}$).
2. Symmetrical output voltage swing around a mid-rail quiescent bias point ($V_{\text{OUT}} \approx 0.40\text{ V}$ on a $0.80\text{ V}$ supply).
3. Dominant-pole frequency compensation via an explicit Miller capacitor ($C_C = 1.0\text{ pF}$), establishing a clean $-20\text{ dB/decade}$ integrator-like roll-off and a Unity-Gain Bandwidth (GBW) exceeding $4.5\text{ MHz}$.

> [!NOTE]
> Circuit B is an intermediate building block representing the second gain stage of a multi-stage operational amplifier. It is **not** by itself a complete operational amplifier.

## 4. Biasing Mechanism and Headroom Allocation

### A. PMOS Current Mirror Bias ($M_9, R_{\text{PB}}, M_8$)
The PMOS current reference is formed by diode-connected $M_9$ loaded by $R_{\text{PB}}$:
$$I_{\text{REF}} = \frac{V_{\text{BP}}}{R_{\text{PB}}} \approx \frac{0.351\text{ V}}{175\text{ k}\Omega} = 2.005\text{ µA}$$
With $(W/L)_9 = 8.0\text{ µm} / 1.0\text{ µm}$ and $K_p' = 50\text{ µA/V}^2$:
$$V_{ov9} = |V_{\text{GS9}}| - |V_{tp}| \approx \sqrt{\frac{2 I_{\text{REF}}}{K_p' (W/L)}} \approx \sqrt{\frac{4.01\text{ µA}}{400\text{ µA/V}^2}} \approx 100\text{ mV}$$
Hence, $V_{\text{BP}} = V_{\text{DD}} - (|V_{tp}| + V_{ov9}) = 0.80\text{ V} - (0.35\text{ V} + 0.099\text{ V}) = 0.351\text{ V}$.  
Since $M_8$ is identical in aspect ratio to $M_9$ and shares gate voltage $V_{\text{BP}}$, it sources $I_{\text{D8}} \approx 2.00\text{ µA}$ into node `OUT`.

### B. NMOS Gate Bias Tuning ($R_1, R_2$)
For $M_7$ with $(W/L)_7 = 4.0\text{ µm} / 1.0\text{ µm}$ and $K_n' = 150\text{ µA/V}^2$:
$$V_{ov7} = V_{\text{GS7}} - V_{tn} \approx \sqrt{\frac{2 I_{\text{D7}}}{K_n' (W/L)}} = \sqrt{\frac{4.00\text{ µA}}{600\text{ µA/V}^2}} \approx 81.6\text{ mV}$$
The required gate voltage is $V_{\text{GATE}} = 0.35\text{ V} + 0.081\text{ V} = 0.4309\text{ V}$.

The initial starting value of $R_1 = 170\text{ k}\Omega$ with $R_2 = 200\text{ k}\Omega$ set $V_{\text{GATE}} = 0.4324\text{ V}$. Due to the very large output resistance ($r_{o7} \parallel r_{o8} \approx 5.1\text{ M}\Omega$) and stage gain ($\sim -252\text{ V/V}$), this $+1.5\text{ mV}$ offset pulled $V(\text{OUT})$ down to $75.7\text{ mV}$, driving $M_7$ into triode.  
By adjusting $R_1$ to **$171.35\text{ k}\Omega$** ($+0.79\%$ adjustment), $V_{\text{GATE}}$ settled precisely to $0.43086\text{ V}$, centering $V(\text{OUT})$ at **$0.3998\text{ V}$** ($\approx V_{\text{DD}}/2$).

### C. Saturation Headroom Margins
- **NMOS $M_7$:**  
  $V_{\text{DS7}} = 0.400\text{ V} > V_{ov7} = 0.081\text{ V}$. Saturation margin = **$+319\text{ mV}$**.
- **PMOS $M_8$:**  
  $|V_{\text{DS8}}| = 0.400\text{ V} > |V_{ov8}| = 0.099\text{ V}$. Saturation margin = **$+301\text{ mV}$**.
- **Symmetrical Dynamic Range:** Linear swing extends from $0.10\text{ V}$ to $0.70\text{ V}$ ($600\text{ mV}_{\text{pk-pk}}$ on a $0.80\text{ V}$ rail).

## 5. Miller Compensation vs. Mathematical Integrator

### A. Miller Compensation Principle
Capacitor $C_C$ connects between the high-gain inverting node `OUT` and the gate of $M_7$. Due to the Miller effect, the effective input capacitance is multiplied by the stage voltage gain:
$$C_{\text{in,eff}} = C_C \cdot (1 + |A_v|) \approx 1\text{ pF} \cdot (1 + 252) \approx 253\text{ pF}$$
This creates a dominant pole at:
$$f_{p1} \approx \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot R_{\text{out}} \cdot C_{\text{eff}}} \approx 15.6\text{ kHz}$$
For all frequencies between $f_{p1}$ ($15.6\text{ kHz}$) and the unity-gain frequency $f_u$ ($4.54\text{ MHz}$), the stage exhibits an exact $-20\text{ dB/decade}$ magnitude slope and an approximate $90^\circ$ phase lag from midband inversion ($180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ$).

### B. Distinction from an Ideal Integrator
An ideal mathematical integrator has a transfer function $H(s) = \frac{1}{s \tau}$ with an infinite DC gain ($A_{v0} = \infty$) and a pole at $s = 0$.  
In contrast, this physical Miller stage is a **lossy integrator**:
- Below $f_{p1}$ ($< 15.6\text{ kHz}$), the finite output impedances $r_{o7}$ and $r_{o8}$ dominate, clamping the gain to a finite value ($A_{v0} \approx 48.04\text{ dB}$).
- It behaves as an integrator **only over the frequency band** $f_{p1} \ll f \ll f_u$.
- When used inside a two-stage op-amp, this dominant pole accomplishes pole splitting, pushing high-frequency nondominant poles past GBW to guarantee stability and high phase margin.

## 9. Limitations of Level-1 Models

The simulation results use generic Level-1 (Shichman-Hodges) analytical MOSFET models:
1. **Velocity Saturation & Short-Channel Effects:** Level 1 assumes carrier mobility is independent of electric field. Sub-micron processes experience velocity saturation, which lowers transconductance $g_m$.
2. **Subthreshold Conduction:** Weak-inversion exponential current conduction is not modeled.
3. **Drain-Induced Barrier Lowering (DIBL):** Threshold voltage $V_{\text{th}}$ is assumed constant with $V_{\text{DS}}$, whereas in modern processes output resistance is degraded by DIBL.
4. **Physical PDK Migration:** Prior to silicon tape-out, this circuit must be resimulated using BSIM4 models from a target foundry (e.g. TSMC 180nm or SkyWater 130nm).
